YOUR LEARNING PATH

Courses

How the piano training progresses

The lessons follow a beginner adaptation of Michael Clark’s Baylor Piano Basics: keyboard orientation and relaxed movement, reading and rhythm, major patterns and scales, broken chords and harmony, then minor keys and longer two-hand studies. Every lesson is played on the piano with the same live score and feedback.

Each lesson has three playable stages: a short introduction, a longer connected exercise, and a musical application. Start slowly. Replay until the movement feels comfortable, then continue. Info explains the skill and shows the hand position; it is optional.

This is an independent beginner training adaptation, not the full Baylor university course. Educational pathway and new guides adapted from Baylor Piano Basics by Michael Clark, Baylor University, under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. Changes include beginner sequencing, original exercises, simplified playable arrangements, explanations and diagrams. New educational adaptations share that license; existing third-party music retains its own rights. No Baylor endorsement.

About these studies

Recognizable classical and traditional openings, from first notes to advanced pitch studies. Every theme includes listening, live notation and a full-page sheet. Learning speed uses 75% of the suggested normal study tempo. Tempi are interpretation guides. Rhythm-mapped arrangements include short notes and rests; unmapped entries are labelled pitch studies and do not receive timing penalties. Two-hand versions use octave doubling. Classical original scores: Mutopia. Games, Pop and Memes include brief unofficial simplified motif studies, not full song scores. The study tempo and rhythm are learning adaptations. Skill labels connect each selection to the course; octave-doubled two-hand studies are coordination practice rather than independent accompaniment.

A QUICK READING GUIDE

The notes are your map.

The upper staff uses treble clef; the lower uses bass clef. Each has five lines. A step to the next line or space moves to the next letter name.

Treble: E4 is the bottom line. Middle C is on a short ledger line below. Bass: C3 is the second space from the bottom. Middle C is on a ledger line above.

Filled note + stem = 1 beat.
Hollow note + stem = 2 beats.
Hollow note without a stem = 4 beats.
A flag = ½ beat. A dot adds half the original value.

Small numbers above notes are fingers. The highlighted column follows the example, then waits for your correct note in practice.

This is an unbarred learning score, with one note at a time. Pitch feedback does not assess rhythm, fingering, or simultaneous chords.

BEFORE YOU PLAY

Make yourself at home.

For Bluetooth MIDI, open this live HTTPS page in Chrome and tap Connect & pair piano. Select your piano or its BLE MIDI adapter, then play a test note. Close other MIDI test pages first. For acoustic input, select Microphone and place the device near the middle of your piano.

  1. Sit comfortably, with forearms roughly level with the keys. Let your fingers curve naturally.
  2. Find the groups of two black keys. C is the white key immediately to their left. C4 is middle C, near the center of the piano.
  3. Enable the microphone and allow access. Play one note at a time, without the sustain pedal. Release each note before the next.
  4. Use headphones for the example and metronome so the microphone hears only your piano.

Finger numbers, either hand:
1 thumb · 2 index · 3 middle · 4 ring · 5 little finger.

This app checks single-note pitch and sequence, not posture, fingering, chords, or rhythm accuracy. The beat counts are practice guidance. Room noise, piano tuning, and device microphones can affect detection.

Microphone troubleshooting & settings

Android Chrome: Open the HTTPS app link directly in Chrome. In Android Settings → Apps → Chrome → Permissions → Microphone, allow access while using the app. Then in Chrome ⋮ → Settings → Site settings → Microphone, enable “Sites can ask” and allow this site if blocked. Reload and tap Enable microphone. Check that Android’s Microphone access privacy switch is on.

Open this file directly in a browser. If microphone access is blocked, serve it through localhost or HTTPS and open it in its own tab. Check browser microphone permissions and system input settings. Use Chrome or Edge as a practical starting point.
